# Field layout workspace

Version 0.2.1 uses one **Field layout** stage for sensors and crop beds: Irradiance → Field layout → Methods & export. The complete workflow has nine stages. Saved navigation from the former sensor/crop stages converges to Field layout; saved publication navigation remains at Methods & export. Geometry, environment and calculation inputs retain the existing guided workflow. A successful calculation collapses the input panel into a narrow, labeled-on-hover icon rail; the expand button restores detailed controls. Sensors and crop beds can also be designed before a calculation, with an explicit missing-light-results message.

## Interaction strategy

- The toolbar offers Select / move, Add sensor, Add crop bed, Undo and Find item. Drag an add button onto the map, or click it and then click a receiver cell. New items open their editable card immediately. Crop beds start at one receiver cell and use the selected catalog crop.
- Click an item to edit its information. A sensor card includes identity, instrument type, receiver coordinates, independent installation height/depth, treatment, replicate, model, logger, channel, azimuth, tilt and notes. A bed card includes identity, crop, cultivar, receiver extent, treatment, replicate and notes, plus saved-layout light statistics and reservation overlaps. Apply commits the draft; Cancel or Escape discards it. Delete is undoable.
- Drag a sensor or bed to move it. Drag any selected bed corner to resize it; the opposite corner stays fixed. Movement snaps to receiver cells, and bed edges snap to cell boundaries. Items remain inside the grid; a bed cannot flip or exceed the existing 100 m dimension limit. Sensor height/depth is independent of map movement. Sensors are point instruments, so their display markers have no physical resize handles.
- Top-down is the clearest placement view; oblique editing also projects pointer positions onto the ground plane. Profile supports selecting/editing coordinates, but not planar dragging. Choosing an add tool from Profile opens Top-down. Existing pan/orbit/zoom is retained through edits and layer changes.
- Focus an item and use arrow keys to move one cell; Shift + arrows resize a bed from its northeast corner. Enter opens its card. Escape cancels an in-progress gesture. Find item provides direct access to overlapping or densely grouped instruments. Pointer capture also supports touch gestures; keyboard and coordinate fields provide alternatives.
- Undo retains the last 40 committed layout states during the current page session, covering additions, moves, resizes, metadata changes and deletions, including expanded-sidebar field edits. Pointer cancellation does not create a history entry. Undo restores field layouts only and normalizes them to the current receiver grid; it does not revert geometry or weather. Import clears history. History and calculated results are not persisted by browser autosave across reloads. Version 0.3.0 [project packages](PROJECT-PACKAGES.md) preserve matching results and restore them on import; Undo history remains session-only.
- Desktop cards anchor near the selected item. On narrow screens they become a bottom sheet with a scrollable field area and visible action buttons. Map tools remain outside the drawing so they do not obscure its light values. Engineering callouts are suppressed during field editing; selection outlines and handles provide the relevant guidance.
- Field IDs use white-backed 12 px tags: orange for sensors and green for beds. Sensor IDs sit near their dots, and bed IDs prefer the bed edges. A shared screen-space placement routine keeps tags inside the drawing and separated from each other, sensor dots and the north indicator. Selection gets priority. Long IDs are shortened in tags while titles, cards and tables retain the complete ID. Crowded views omit excess tags rather than shrink or overlap them; Find item and zoom provide access to every item. Labels are clickable during editing and read-only on Methods & export.

The heatmap is reused while layouts change, and dragging previews remain local to the display until release. Sensor/crop edits never enter scientific occluders or invalidate a calculation. Plot statistics are computed from the cells actually occupied by the saved bed. Ground reservations remain optional legend layers and overlaps are reported without forcibly relocating research plots. Completed irradiance initially retains the clean modules/light-map display; entering the sensor/crop stages or selecting an item reveals the relevant field layers.

## Controlled crop identities

The bundled catalog contains **426 crop choices representing 309 accepted botanical identities**. It covers cereals, pulses, vegetables, fruits, nuts, oil/fiber/sugar crops, herbs, spices, forage and cover crops. Common crop uses can share a species, such as different uses of alfalfa. This is a broad predefined agricultural catalog, not a claim to enumerate every cultivated plant or cultivar.

The starting crop names come from the [FAO World Programme for the Census of Agriculture crop list](https://www.fao.org/4/a0135e/A0135E10.htm), supplemented with additional agricultural crops and common aliases. FAO describes its botanical/common-name list in the [WCA classification documentation](https://www.fao.org/statistics/caliper/classifications/wca/en). Crop-use names are mapped to botanical identities; horticultural groups and cultivars remain separate from species taxonomy.

Botanical names were checked against the [GBIF species API](https://techdocs.gbif.org/en/openapi/v1/species). Only exact matches resolving to accepted species, subspecies or varieties enter the catalog. Synonyms resolve to their accepted record, while the requested name remains searchable. Names rejected as fuzzy, higher-rank, doubtful or otherwise unresolved require an explicit seed correction. GBIF acceptance is a named, dated taxonomic treatment; it does not imply unanimous botanical agreement.

Taxonomy attribution: GBIF Secretariat (2023). *GBIF Backbone Taxonomy*. Checklist dataset [doi:10.15468/39omei](https://doi.org/10.15468/39omei), accessed via GBIF.org on September 10, 2026. Taxonomic data are adapted under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The catalog preserves accepted names, authorship, family and record keys; application-specific common names, aliases and crop-choice IDs are curated mappings. Catalog version: `2026-09-10`.

Typing searches common names, botanical names and aliases immediately, without a network request. Users must select an offered entry; arbitrary search text cannot become a crop identity. Cultivar / variety remains a separate free-text field because the catalog is not a cultivar registry. Every bed stores its crop-choice ID, common name, accepted botanical name, scientific name with authorship, family, GBIF key/link and catalog version.

Version-1 studies remain compatible. Unambiguous existing names acquire catalog identities. Unrecognized or ambiguous imported names retain their original text and layout and are visibly flagged for selection; the application does not invent a botanical assignment or silently delete a bed. New beds and applied bed edits require a catalog selection. Reports explicitly mark unresolved legacy records. Known catalog IDs always supply canonical identity fields rather than trusting imported metadata.

The reproducible maintenance command is `node scripts/build-crop-catalog.mjs`. It reads `src/data/crop-seeds.json`, uses the checked-in raw responses in `docs/crop-taxonomy-validation.json`, and writes `src/data/crop-catalog.json`. Uncached names use HTTPS requests through `curl`; no key or backend is required. This command never runs as part of the normal build or browser session. Updating taxonomy requires a reviewed snapshot/version update; historical evidence remains in the validation file.

## Publication record

The compact methods report adds a four-column bed-identity table: bed/common crop, botanical name/cultivar, family, and taxonomy record. A separate compact layout/light table carries coordinates, dimensions, treatment, replicate, sampled light and reserved-area overlaps. Notes and botanical authorship are retained. JSON and CSV include each bed's complete crop identity and catalog version; methods metadata includes the taxonomy citation and license. Botanical taxonomy does not supply crop-specific light-response or yield assumptions.

Methods & export and every report figure omit engineering dimensions, angle witnesses, row callouts and numbered sensor leaders. Field ID tags use the same placement routine as the map. Compact report figures refer to the complete field tables rather than repeating a long instrument key below each image; standalone figures retain a full field key. Scale bars, north indicators, ground references, ground-zone legends and all methods parameters remain available. Geometry-input stages retain their instructional engineering callouts.

## Verification scope

Automated checks cover rotated-cell movement and clamping, all four resize anchors, dimension limits, unchanged numerical keys, identity normalization, catalog integrity against recorded exact/accepted matches, escaped report/CSV metadata, mandatory keyboard crop selection, pointer cancellation/drop handling, and post-solve workspace navigation.

Live local browser checks completed a WebGPU light calculation, confirmed automatic sidebar collapse, moved a sensor without changing its installation height, resized a bed with a fixed opposite corner, observed updated plot statistics, selected a crop by botanical-name search, dragged a new sensor from the toolbar, and restored the original layout through Undo. A 390 × 844 viewport had no horizontal document overflow; the crop editor's footer remained visible after the bottom-sheet correction. These checks cover one browser/runtime. Physical touch hardware, other browsers, assistive technologies and physical print pagination remain broader release checks. WebGL fallback drawings retain coordinate-based editing through the sidebar/cards; direct graphical dragging requires the interactive renderer.
